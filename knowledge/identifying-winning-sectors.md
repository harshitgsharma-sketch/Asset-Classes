## Why sectors matter

There are over 5,500 listed companies but only a few dozen sectors, so thinking in sectors makes stock picking simpler. It also lowers the chance of holding a stock that goes nowhere while the market rises. Different sectors lead in different years: real estate, telecom, auto and capital goods in 2024; IT, healthcare and consumption in 2020; energy in 2016.

## Why sectors rotate: the business cycle

| Phase | Sectors that tend to lead |
|---|---|
| Recovery and expansion (activity rebounding, credit growth and capex rising, rates falling, earnings improving) | Financials, consumer discretionary, real estate, metals |
| Peak (activity stabilising, rates rising, earnings strong) | Financials, consumer discretionary, energy, metals |
| Slowdown and contraction | IT, consumer staples, pharma, utilities (defensives) |

## Four ways to pick sectors

1. **Track economic and business indicators:** economic activity, credit growth, capex, interest rates and corporate earnings tell you roughly where the cycle is.
2. **Growth:** compare a sector's revenue growth with nominal GDP growth (9.9% for FY25). Above it in May 2025: realty, consumer durables, finance, capital goods, healthcare. Below it: metals, FMCG, utilities, oil and gas, IT. Earnings beats against estimates also flag outperformers (IT and metals after FY21 results; PSUs and telecom after FY23).
3. **Returns and valuation:**
   - Compare each sector's 1-year return with the Nifty 500. Ride the leaders for momentum, or buy laggards as a contrarian or value bet.
   - Compare each sector's P/B *premium to the Nifty 500* with its own median. P/B is used because it is steadier than P/E. Examples: pharma at a 36.6% premium against an 18.8% median looked expensive; IT at 93.2% against a 104.3% median looked cheap.
   - Caveat: valuations reflect expectations, so combine them with the business outlook.
4. **Long-term "sunrise" cycles:** semiconductors, electronics manufacturing (EMS), EVs, green energy; themes like China+1, Make in India, financialisation, urbanisation; secular stories in telecom (ARPU), consumer discretionary and healthcare. Rotate between segments inside these. Shorter cycles come from policy (defence localisation) and seasonality (AC makers sell more in the March and June quarters; also hotels, chemicals, fertilisers, jewellery, power, cement).

## Summary advice

Sector rotation sits between fundamental and tactical investing. The simplest form is to lean towards cyclicals when the economy is strong and towards defensives when it is slowing, gradually rather than all at once. It takes time and effort. Index funds or ETFs are a simpler way to take sector bets, and mutual fund investors can leave it to their fund manager.
