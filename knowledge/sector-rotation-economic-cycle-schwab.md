# Sector rotation through the economic cycle (the classic US sequence)

- **Source:** [Sector Rotation: How the Economic Cycle Affects Stocks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C0BK_Mip9oM), Charles Schwab (YouTube). 6 minutes, English. Published 13 May 2025.
- **Added:** 2026-11-22

## Main point

The economic cycle drives corporate profits, and profits drive stock prices. Different sectors are sensitive to different stages, so investors rotate between them as the cycle turns.

## The textbook order

| Stage | Sector investors rotate into | Why |
|---|---|---|
| End of recession | Financials (banks, brokers, insurers) | Interest rates favour them; investors anticipate recovery |
| Early recovery | Technology | Businesses invest in productivity |
| Recovery gathers pace | Consumer discretionary (autos, housing, retail) | Consumer confidence and spending rise |
| Mid growth | Industrials and basic materials | Production expands to meet demand |
| Late growth | Energy | Demand from transporting goods |
| Early recession | Consumer staples (food, drinks, household goods) | People keep buying essentials |
| Recession continues | Utilities | Bills still get paid |
| Recession worsens | Healthcare | Spending regardless of the economy |
| Tail end of recession | Services (waste management, staffing) | Then the cycle restarts with financials |

## How to use it

- Watch price trends by sector. In a recovery, look for new uptrends in industrials and materials; in a recession, in utilities and healthcare.
- Look for confirmation, because rotations are hard to predict.
- Hold or add when a sector comes into favour. When one falls out of favour, raise stop-losses or sell to lock in gains.
- Watch at least one full cycle before acting on it.
