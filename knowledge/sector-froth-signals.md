# Sector froth: fund launches, IPO waves and capex booms

- **Source:** Desk research, 2026-11-22: academic papers, AMFI data and press coverage. Sources are linked inline and listed at the end.
- **Added:** 2026-11-22
- **Tags:** research, froth, thematic funds, NFOs, IPO waves, capital cycle, India
- **Relevant models here:** Nifty sectors oscillator (Size module: small/mid MF inflows, IPO issuance; Sector cycle bucket); [backlog](board-backlog.md) items 2 and 17.

> Not investment advice. Several figures come from press summaries of AMFI data and are labelled as such.

## The idea

When a sector or theme gets popular, three kinds of supply arrive: new funds to buy it, new shares (IPOs and QIPs) to sell into it, and new capacity built to profit from it. All three tend to peak near the top and are followed by weak returns. They are also visible *before* earnings turn, which makes them useful exit-side signals.

## 1. New funds and ETFs for a theme

- **[Ben-David, Franzoni, Kim and Moussawi, "Competition for Attention in the ETF Space"](https://www.nber.org/papers/w28369)** (Review of Financial Studies, 2023):
  - Specialized (thematic) ETFs are launched when their underlying stocks are already overvalued.
  - Over their first five years they lose about 30% in risk-adjusted terms.
  - The authors read this as fund providers catering to investors' extrapolative beliefs.
- **[Morningstar "Mind the Gap"](https://www.morningstar.com/business/insights/research/mind-the-gap):**
  - Sector equity funds have the widest gap between the fund's return and what its investors actually earn, because money arrives late and leaves early.
  - The gap was −2.6% a year over the 10 years to 2023 ([2024 report](https://marketing.morningstar.com/content/cs-assets/v3/assets/blt9415ea4cc4157833/blt5d1d34a59bd78a2c/680a4bd8c4d2e877fdb4b354/Mind_the_Gap_2024.pdf)).
  - In the 2025 edition, investors earned 7.0% a year against the funds' 8.5%.
- **India's 2024 thematic boom** (AMFI data as reported in the press):
  - Sectoral/thematic funds were the largest equity inflow category in FY24, at over ₹46,000 crore.
  - Net inflows hit a record ₹22,351 crore in June 2024, largely from NFOs ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/mutual-funds/amfi-may-2024-data-sectoralthematic-fund-inflows-surged)).
  - Thematic NFOs raised about ₹79,219 crore in calendar 2024, about two-thirds of all NFO money ([IIFL](https://www.indiainfoline.com/blog/thematic-funds-propel-calendar-2024-nfo-flows-to-1-19-trillion)).
  - Category AUM rose from ₹1.73 lakh crore (Mar 2023) to ₹4.62 lakh crore (Nov 2024).
- **What followed:**
  - Inflows dropped 97% to ₹170 crore in March 2025 ([Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/223932/mutual-fund-inflows-sectoral-funds-march-2025/)), and interest waned through 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/investor-interest-declines-in-sectoral-thematic-mutual-funds-2025-125061201131_1.html)).
  - By December 2025, thematic funds had averaged −1.7% over one year and −16.3% over three years (Value Research data via [Financial Express](https://www.magzter.com/ja/stories/newspaper/Financial-Express-Pune/SECTORAL-THEMATIC-FUNDS-UNDERPERFORM-MARKETS)).
  - Note: the three-year figure covers launches made before the peak too.
- **Data source:** [AMFI monthly data](https://www.amfiindia.com/research-information/amfi-monthly) is free and published every month as PDF and Excel. It includes net inflows for the "Sectoral/Thematic" category and NFO collections.

## 2. IPO waves

- **[Da, Jagannathan and Shen, "Building Castles in the Air: Evidence from Industry IPO Waves"](https://www.nber.org/papers/w18555)** (NBER):
  - Firms that list in an industry IPO wave have average future returns below the risk-free rate.
  - Among the high-growth firms, those with low gross margins underperform wave peers by 0.92% a month (about 12% a year) over the next four years.
- **[Pástor and Veronesi](https://users.nber.org/~confer/2003/apf03/pastor.pdf):** IPO waves follow high market returns and come before low ones.
- **India examples** (from the [sector rotation note](sector-rotation-7-indian-bull-runs.md)): internet IPOs in 2021 (Paytm, Nykaa, PB Fintech, Eternal); agrochemical formulators in 2021–22; solar in 2024–25; transformers and co-working spaces more recently.

## 3. Capex and capacity booms

- **[Titman, Wei and Xie](https://www.nber.org/papers/w9951.pdf):** firms that sharply raise capital investment go on to earn negative benchmark-adjusted returns, separately from long-term reversal and equity issuance effects.
- **[Hoberg and Phillips](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14290.pdf):** in growth industries, high industry valuation, high investment and heavy new financing predict lower returns. Their examples include past telecom and railway booms.
- A Columbia working paper (Sadka et al.) finds industries with high capacity utilisation *and* high sales growth underperform.
- This is the empirical side of Marathon's **capital cycle** (see the [cyclical valuation note](valuing-cyclical-sectors.md)).

## How to read the signals together

| Signal | Where to see it (India) | Frequency | Reads as froth when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sectoral/thematic fund net inflows and NFO collections | AMFI monthly | Monthly | At a multi-year high, or a cluster of NFOs on one theme |
| Thematic NFOs by theme (defence, PSU, manufacturing, healthcare…) | AMFI / AMC filings | Monthly | Several launches on the same theme within a few months |
| Industry IPOs and QIPs | NSE, Prime Database | Monthly | Several in one industry within 12 months |
| Announced capacity additions | Company presentations, industry bodies | Quarterly | Planned capacity well above demand growth (transformers 2025–26) |
