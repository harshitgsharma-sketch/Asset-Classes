## The question

Why does leadership in the market keep changing between sectors? How can you tell whether a sector's run will be short or long, and what tools show a change in leadership while it is happening?

**Opening example:** from 2010 to 2019, Asian Paints (high return on capital) beat BHEL easily. In the cycle that followed, BHEL beat Asian Paints, even though Asian Paints still earns a 20%+ return on capital and BHEL earns about 8–10%. A second example: graphite electrode makers (Graphite India, HEG) had a huge profit boom in 2017–18, and the stocks did badly for years afterwards.

## Seven Indian bull runs and their leaders

| Period | Leaders | How it ended |
|---|---|---|
| 1999–2003 | IT, telecom, media | Dot-com bust and the Ketan Parekh scam; leading stocks fell 84–98% |
| 2003–08 | Capital goods, power, real estate, metals (L&T up about 730%, BHEL about 580%) | 2008 financial crisis; Nifty fell about 58% |
| 2009–13 | Autos and banks first, then FMCG, pharma, IT | Slow run; index fell about 28% |
| 2013–20 | Private banks, NBFCs, consumer brands (Bajaj Finance up about 1,400%) | IL&FS crisis, small-cap collapse, then Covid |
| 2020–21 | Metals, IT, new-age tech, pharma | Nifty fell about 17% |
| 2022–Mar 2025 | Public-sector companies, defence (defence index up about 333%), capex and capital goods | Elections, higher taxes, rich valuations |
| Mar 2025 to now | Capital markets, public-sector banks, defence, autos, pharma | Not yet a proper bull run, in his view |

**Takeaways**

- Every bull run is led by a few sectors.
- No sector leads forever. The laggards of one decade often lead the next.
- After each bust, a different group takes over.
- Be humble about whatever is winning now. In a bull run one sector's P/E can go from 10 to 100 while another's falls from 50 to 10–20.

## The four "engines" of a sector cycle

### 1. Psychology (euphoria)

Warning signs that a sector, or the whole market, is euphoric:

- **A wave of IPOs or share sales (QIPs) in one sector.** Examples: solar in 2024–25; the internet IPOs (Paytm, Nykaa, PB Fintech, Eternal) in 2021; agrochemical formulators in 2021–22. Big IPOs tend to cluster near a sector's peak.
- **Market breadth:** 90–95% of listed stocks trading above their 200-day moving average. That happened in early 2024.
- **Parabolic move:** price more than **70% above its 150-day EMA**. Examples: silver from December 2025 to March 2026; IRCTC, IEX, Schaeffler and Deepak Nitrite in 2021.
- **Narrative:** everyone repeating the same story, like "India's decade" in 2023–24.

### 2. The capital cycle

From Edward Chancellor's book *Capital Returns*. The loop goes: high returns, capital floods in, capacity overshoots, returns collapse, capital leaves, supply tightens, high returns again.

Where he places sectors (late 2026):

| Stage | Sectors |
|---|---|
| High returns | Hotels (15–20% return on capital) |
| Capital flooding in | Transformers (new IPOs; Voltamp margins down from about 19.5% to 15.5%, Shilchar from about 31% to 20%); solar; smart meters |
| Turning down | Cables (new entrants; Polycab, KEI, RR Kabel near stage 4); co-working spaces |
| Declining or consolidating | Paints (lower highs since 2022); water EPC (down about 42%) |

**How to track it:** IPO and capex announcements in the sector, plus a custom sector index on which you apply stage analysis or Dow theory (higher highs vs. lower highs).

### 3. Valuation-led bubbles

The future gets paid for in advance. These deflate **slowly and painfully**, often over about five years.

- DMart: P/E fell from about 226 to about 73. Earnings grew, but the stock is still down over five years.
- Paints and FMCG had negative or flat five-year returns.
- Trent traded at about 20 times sales in 2024.
- **The trigger to watch is a slowing *rate of change*** in growth (for example Trent's revenue growth going from 50–60% down to 15–20%), **or new alternatives appearing**, as quick commerce did for DMart.

### 4. Earnings-led bubbles

Four to five years of an industry's growth gets packed into 12–18 months. Industries normally grow at two to three times GDP; growth at five to six times GDP usually borrows from the future.

- Diagnostics after Covid: the sector index went from about 22 to 82, then fell back to about 39 as private-equity money and new entrants (Tata 1mg and others) cut prices. Dr Lal's revenue growth normalised from 19–32% to 10–15%.
- He asks the same question about AI and Nvidia's low P/E: if the data-centre build-out slows, today's earnings may not last.

## How long cycles last: three timeframes

| Timeframe | Driver | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Long** (5+ years) | Structural change, or a long bear market that destroyed supply. "The bigger the bear market, the bigger the bull market." | Power and capital goods, HVDC transmission, insulators, hotels (after about 14 dull years), transformers (about 10 dull years, then about 6 good ones). He expects power to be overbuilt around 2031–33. |
| **Medium** (2–4 years) | Somewhat structural, but cycles with the capital cycle | Diagnostics, hospitals (risk of price wars in tier-1 cities), wealth managers, CDMOs (strong for another 24–36 months, then valuation risk) |
| **Short** (1–2 years) | Commodity-driven | Graphite electrodes, sugar, optical fibre. He thinks transformer profits could shrink within 12–18 months; bushings have 24–30 months; insulators 4–6 years. |

Before investing, study the sector's history, including in other countries. For example, solar overcapacity bankrupted companies in China and the US.

## Tools for spotting a rotation (shown on StockScans)

1. **A sector's P/E against its long-term range.** Nifty was at about 19–20× (average or slightly below), while earnings growth was about 21%, the fastest in six quarters; he thinks another 10–15% rise is possible. Nifty IT was at about 18×, against peaks of about 40× (2021) and 35× (2024), and a pre-Covid range of 15–20×.
2. **Relative strength (RS) score.** An index or industry **above 75** (top 25% of the market) goes on the watchlist; then study the companies inside it that are outperforming. Recent examples: capital markets (84), India internet (76), public-sector banks (top 6%), marine ports and dredging, exchanges, music licensing, watches.
3. **Custom sector indices with stage analysis or Dow theory.** Look for a sector breaking into an uptrend (stage 2), or a pattern of lower highs. Examples: CDMOs up about 50–60% in six months; hospitals recovering after better Q2 profits.
4. **Results across the whole sector.** Are several companies growing operating profit or expanding margins together? Sector profits tend to rise and fall together, because "liquidity creates fundamentals, and fundamentals create charts." Examples: margins falling together across transformers and smart meters; order books growing together in electronics manufacturing (Syrma, Centum and others).
