# Sector investing playbook: rules distilled from the knowledge base

- **Source:** Synthesis of every note in this folder as of 2026-11-22. Each rule links to the evidence behind it.
- **Added:** 2026-11-22
- **Tags:** playbook, rules, sector rotation, synthesis
- **Relevant models here:** all sector-level readings on the board.

> A working summary to be revised as notes are added. It reflects what the evidence collected so far supports for an INR investor holding for 5 years or less. Not investment advice.

## What tends to work

1. **Buy sectors that are cheap against their own history, not against the market.** Returns on capital by industry persist for decades, so each sector has its own "normal" multiple ([cyclical valuation](valuing-cyclical-sectors.md#3-returns-on-capital-by-industry-last-for-decades)). The board's sector scores are built this way.
2. **Lean against 1–3 year leadership.** Last year's winners beat the Nifty again only 47% of the time; ranked by 12-month strength, leaders trailed laggards by about 6.5 pp the next year in 2016–26 ([India facts](india-sector-facts-2016-2026.md), [research](sector-rotation-research-evidence.md#2-long-term-industry-reversal)).
3. **Value cyclicals on book value or normalised earnings.** A low P/E on peak margins is a trap ([cyclicals](valuing-cyclical-sectors.md#1-for-cyclicals-a-low-pe-is-often-a-warning)).
4. **Watch supply, not just demand.** Clusters of IPOs, QIPs, thematic NFOs and capacity announcements in one sector come before weak returns ([froth](sector-froth-signals.md), [capital cycle](valuing-cyclical-sectors.md#2-the-capital-cycle-marathon-asset-management)).
5. **Size each sector position for a 60–70% fall and a 3–5 year wait.** That is what PSU banks, metals, autos and realty did in 2016–26 ([drawdowns](india-sector-facts-2016-2026.md#3-sector-drawdowns-are-deep-and-long)).
6. **Use index funds or ETFs for sector exposure.** Most active Indian funds trail their benchmarks over 10 years (SPIVA), and investors in sector funds earn the least of all fund categories, because they buy late and sell early ([froth](sector-froth-signals.md#1-new-funds-and-etfs-for-a-theme)).

## What tends not to work

- **Buying the theme everyone is launching funds for.** Thematic ETFs lost about 30% risk-adjusted over their first five years; India's 2024 thematic NFO boom was followed by −16% average three-year returns for the category ([froth](sector-froth-signals.md)).
- **Rotating by business-cycle phase.** Even with perfect foresight of US recessions the gain was about 2% a year before costs, and nil after costs and timing errors ([research](sector-rotation-research-evidence.md#3-business-cycle-rotation)).
- **Chasing a parabolic move.** Prices often kept rising after the 70%-above-150-day-EMA mark, but with violent swings; it marks a late stage, not a top. Don't buy into it, and don't treat it alone as a sell signal ([backlog: built](board-backlog.md#built)).
- **Treating sector momentum as a free lunch.** No usable 1–6 month sector momentum in India 2016–26, and momentum strategies crash after market falls ([research](sector-rotation-research-evidence.md)).

## A checklist before acting on a sector score

1. Score in Buy (≤35)? Check the **relative** score too: cheap on its own *and* against the market is the strongest case.
2. Cyclical family (PSU Bank, Metal, Energy, Realty, Auto)? Check margins aren't at a cycle peak.
3. Any froth: several IPOs or NFOs on this theme in the last 12 months, or a parabolic flag?
4. How far is the sector below its high, and for how long? Deep and long is normal for cyclicals; recent and shallow means little has been priced in.
5. What is the capital cycle doing: new capacity coming (wait) or capacity leaving and consolidating (better)?
6. Size so that a 60% fall is survivable, and stage entries when the macro filter is high (the board's staging rule).
7. Write down the exit trigger now: score above 65, the relative score turning rich, or the growth rate of revenue slowing for two quarters.
