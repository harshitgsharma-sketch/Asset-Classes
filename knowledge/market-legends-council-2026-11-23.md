## Round 1: opinions

### Jim Simons: GO WITH CONDITIONS
*"Show me the data."* Of the board's 13 engines, one has a point-in-time back-test: the size-rotation model. It shows a real signal (correlation +0.43 with next-year midcap excess return) but only about 0.7 percentage points a year of edge, close to none after 2017. Every other score rests on judgment anchors, so treat each band as a hypothesis, not an edge. The short-term-drawdown list is a screen nobody has tested: a 15% fall is a description, not a signal.
- **Questions:** Which other engines can be back-tested next? What is the hit rate of "Buy" bands 12 months later? Are the stock anchors survivorship-biased?
- **Conditions:** small tilts only, and log every monthly reading so the bands can be scored against outcomes.
- **Lesson cited:** Medallion's early years, when discretion lost money and models had to be tested before being trusted.

### Paul Tudor Jones: GO WITH CONDITIONS
*"Play great defence."* The best risk-reward on the board is boring: an 8% short-term yield with no equity risk while the RBI is hiking. Silver at −62%, gold at −31% and Bitcoin at −50% from their highs are cheap on the board but falling on price, and *losers average losers*: nothing gets bought below its 200-day average until price turns. The Nifty 50 sits 3.7% off its high, so the trend is intact for large caps, not for the cheapest stocks.
- **Questions:** Where is each cheap asset against its 200-day average (the board has this for indices, not for every stock)? What is the stop on each tilt? Who is crowded? (Small-cap funds.)
- **Conditions:** no averaging down on silver, Bitcoin, Amara Raja or the short-term list. Scale in only once price confirms. Write the stop before entering.
- **Lesson cited:** October 1987, when he expected the crash from price and analogue, not from valuation.

### Victor Niederhoffer: GO
*"Count it."* The crowd is all in small and midcaps (43% of inflows) and has fled IT, FMCG, China and silver. The base rate favours the hated over the loved over 3–5 years. IT at 28, Infosys at 29 and ITC at 37 are the contrarian sleeve; small caps at 68 and REITs at 88 are what to fade. **The ghost speaks too:** the covered-call carry (59) is short volatility, which is how he was wiped out in October 1997. Keep it small or out. No leverage anywhere.
- **Questions:** How often has Nifty IT at a bottom-quartile valuation beaten the Nifty over 3 years? Is anything on this book short tail risk?
- **Conditions:** contrarian positions small, staged and unleveraged, with a written ruin limit.
- **Lesson cited:** 27 Oct 1997, when naked S&P puts ended the fund.

### Ray Dalio: GO WITH CONDITIONS
*"He who lives by the crystal ball will eat shattered glass."* India is in the tightening phase of a short-term debt cycle. The board's mix (cheap bonds, fair large caps, rich small caps and US, gold and silver well off their highs) argues for a balanced book across growth and inflation regimes, not one big call. Bonds being cheap and equities fair is the rare moment when diversification costs little.
- **Questions:** Is inflation rising or falling? (The board doesn't show CPI.) How correlated are the board's "cheap" sleeves (China, Brazil, metals, silver: all commodity and EM)?
- **Conditions:** spread tilts across 10 or more lowly correlated sleeves, and keep duration moderate until the RBI stops hiking.
- **Lesson cited:** 1982, when his confident depression call was wrong and he moved to radical diversification.

### Howard Marks: GO WITH CONDITIONS
*"We may never know where we're going, but we'd better have a good idea where we are."* The temperature is uneven. India large caps are lukewarm, with P/E 21 under its median. Small caps, US, Canada, southern Europe and listed REITs are warm-to-hot. IT, FMCG, China, Indonesia, silver and Indian bonds are cold. Move gradually: trim where it's warm, add in steps where it's cold. Credit (AA spreads about 183 bp, score 43) is fair, not distressed. This is no Oaktree-2008 moment.
- **Questions:** Is the consensus on IT ("AI kills services") already in the price? Are forced sellers still selling silver and Bitcoin?
- **Conditions:** add to cold assets in instalments, and be willing to look wrong for a year.
- **Lesson cited:** the "bubble.com" memo, January 2000, written about two months before the top.

### Stanley Druckenmiller: GO WITH CONDITIONS
*"Don't fight the Fed"*, and here that means the RBI. Rates are going up, so long small caps fight liquidity, and the size model's underweight agrees. The fat pitch comes 18–24 months out: when the RBI turns, duration and the beaten-down rate-sensitives re-rate. Until then, collect 8% and keep size small. When the turn shows (a pause, falling yields, FIIs adding), bet bigger.
- **Questions:** What would make the RBI pause? Is FII buying broad or just large banks?
- **Conditions:** no large bets now. Pre-decide the signal that triggers sizing up.
- **Lesson cited:** 2000, when he chased tech he knew was a bubble and took heavy losses.

## Round 2: debate

- **PTJ vs Niederhoffer on silver, Bitcoin and the short-term list.** Niederhoffer: "Down 62% with the crowd gone: the base rate favours a rebound." PTJ: "Your base rates don't pay margin calls. Below the 200-day, I don't own it." *Resolution:* the board's own staging rule for Bitcoin and a price-confirmation entry for silver satisfy both: small, staged, with a stop.
- **Simons vs Marks on temperature.** Simons: "'Warm' is not a number." Marks: "Neither was 2000, and the memo was right." *Resolution:* keep the temperature calls as direction only, and size them from tested signals; so far only the size model is tested.
- **Dalio vs Druckenmiller on concentration.** Druckenmiller: "When the pivot comes, go for the jugular." Dalio: "And if your timing is wrong, as mine was in 1982?" *Resolution:* stay diversified now, and let the pivot signal, not conviction alone, set when to concentrate.
- **Blind spots raised:** Marks reminded Niederhoffer that "oversold can get more oversold" (his own blind spot). Dalio reminded PTJ that strict 200-day rules whipsaw a 5-year holder.

**Kill criteria** (re-convene the council when any of these happen):

**Survival check:** nothing the board suggests needs leverage or short volatility. The worst plausible case (equities −40% as in 2008 or 2020, silver or Bitcoin halving again) is painful but not ruin, provided the tilts stay small and the covered-call carry stays small. Niederhoffer's rule is met.
