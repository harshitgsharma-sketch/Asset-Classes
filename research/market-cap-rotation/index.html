What the model reads from this

Large caps are cheap against their own history and the market is rewarding them. Midcaps are fairly priced, roughly in line with their usual premium. Smallcaps carry a premium about 19 points above their norm while rates are rising and retail flows are crowded into them. Run through the calculator below, the model output is overweight large, close to neutral mid, underweight small, with total equity near its strategic level.

Two decades of cycles

Mechanics

What actually moves money between segments

Relative valuation

The best single predictor over 1–3 years. A study of 2007–2023 data found the Midcap 150 / Nifty 50 P/B ratio was the most robust predictor of midcap excess returns. When it was stretched, forward excess returns were about −17% and never positive in the sample. Use the premium against its own norm, not the raw P/E.

Relative momentum

Leadership trends for 6–18 months before valuation catches up with it. A segment whose price ratio to the Nifty is below its 200-day average is usually still losing ground. Momentum tells you when to act; valuation tells you which way.

Rates and liquidity

Smaller companies borrow more and are priced off domestic liquidity. Easing cycles (2014–17, 2020–21) lifted them. Tightening and credit squeezes (2011–13, 2018) crushed them. Rising 10-yr yields compress the higher multiples first.

Earnings growth gap

SMID premiums hold only while SMID earnings grow clearly faster. When large-cap growth catches up, as in Q2 FY27, the premium has nothing left to justify it. Watch revision breadth as well as growth.

Who is buying

FIIs buy mostly large caps, so FII inflows lift the Nifty first. Domestic SIPs and retail drive the SMID end. When SMID funds take an outsized share of inflows, it is a crowding signal, not a reason to add.

Supply and froth

IPO and QIP floods, SME IPO oversubscription, rising margin funding and SEBI warnings have shown up near every SMID top (2007, 2017, 2024). Liquidity in small caps disappears exactly when you need to sell.

The model

Six pillars, one score per segment

Midcaps and smallcaps are each scored relative to large caps on a −2 to +2 scale. Positive favours the smaller segment; negative favours large caps. Each pillar is clipped to ±2 and weighted. Large caps take whatever weight is left.

30%

A · Relative valuation gap

Premium now = PE_seg / PE_nifty − 1. Normal premium = the same ratio using medians. Score = −(premium now − normal premium) / 10pp. Every 10 points of extra premium costs one point. Run the same calculation on P/B where you have it and average the two.

15%

B · Own-history valuation

Score = −(PE_seg / median − 1) / 10%. Use a blend of 5- and 10-yr medians: the 10-yr median is inflated by the 2020–21 earnings collapse, and the 5-yr median sits inside the recent boom.

20%

C · Relative momentum

Average of two parts: (i) 6-month return of the segment minus the Nifty 50, divided by 7.5pp, and (ii) +1 if the segment/Nifty price ratio is above its 200-day average, −1 if below.

15%

D · Earnings growth gap

Score = (EPS growth_seg − EPS growth_large) / 10pp, using latest-quarter YoY or forward FY consensus. Use the same source for every segment.

10%

E · Rates and liquidity

RBI cutting +1, on hold 0, hiking −1. 10-yr yield change over 6 months: −change / 50bps, capped at ±1. FII net buying −0.5 (lifts large caps first), selling +0.25. Midcaps feel 75% of this pillar; smallcaps feel all of it.

10%

F · Flows and sentiment (contrarian)

SMID funds' share of active equity inflows. Score = −(share − 30%) / 7.5pp. Add judgement for IPO/SME froth or SEBI warnings. Midcaps take 75% of this.

From score to allocation

  • Start with a strategic base that fits your risk tolerance and horizon: conservative 70/20/10, balanced 55/28/17, aggressive 40/35/25 (large/mid/small). Market weight across the Nifty 500 is roughly 70/20/10.
  • Tilt each smaller segment: target = base × (1 + 0.4 × score). A score of −2 cuts the weight by 80%; +2 raises it by 80%. Large caps take the remainder.
  • How much in equity overall: a separate absolute score blends Nifty P/E against its median (50%), the earnings-yield gap to the 10-yr G-sec (30%) and market cap/GDP (20%). Each point moves the equity share 7.5pp from your base, so the range is ±15pp.
  • Hysteresis: act only when a target is 5pp or more away from what you hold. Move a third of the gap per month through STPs. This stops the model churning on noise.
  • Hard guardrails: smallcaps stay at or below 30% of equity in any state. Cut them to the floor if the Smallcap 250 premium to the Nifty is 25pp or more above normal and momentum is negative.

Where it earned its keep

It bought midcaps after crashes (2009, 2013–14) and cut small and midcaps to 15% and 12% by December 2017, a month before the 2018 top. Those calls produced +7.9 points in 2014, +6.7 in 2018 and +3.6 in 2019.

Where it cost money

From 2021, small and midcaps re-rated and stayed expensive on P/B for years. The model sat at 2–5% smallcaps through the 2023–24 boom and lost 3.6, 3.8 and 4.5 points in 2021, 2023 and 2024.

What carried the signal

Valuation did nearly all the work: the relative valuation gap alone correlated +0.44 with next-year midcap excess return. Momentum (−0.09) and the RBI rate stance (+0.01) added nothing at that horizon.

P/E is unusable for smaller caps

NSE's Smallcap 100 P/E runs into the hundreds or goes blank whenever constituents post losses (most of 2016–2020), and Midcap 100 P/E hit 419× in 2020. The backtest values on P/B instead, which stayed clean. On P/E the model lost to the fixed split (12.0% vs 13.6%).

Verdict

The valuation signal is real, but turning it into extra return is hard. The model added about a point a year in 2008–2016 and nothing after 2017, with slightly smaller drawdowns in the later period. Treat it as a risk dial that leans against extremes, not as a timing engine. Over the same period, the base split mattered far more than timing: Midcap 150 alone compounded at 16.6% a year against 11.8% for the Nifty 50, with deeper falls (−46.6% vs −36.7%).

On the backtest's P/B inputs, November 2026 reads midcap −0.83 and smallcap −1.14, which maps to about 82/14/5. That is more defensive than the calculator above, because the Midcap 100's P/B (3.9) now sits above the Nifty 50's (3.1), against a long history of trading below it.

  • Returns use total-return indices for Nifty 50, Midcap 150 and Smallcap 250 from niftyindices.com.
  • Valuation uses Nifty 50, Midcap 100 (from 2005) and Smallcap 100 (from 2011) P/B, because Midcap 150 and Smallcap 250 valuation history only starts in April 2016. Smallcap scores begin in February 2014; before that, smallcaps sit at base weight.
  • Rolling 10-year medians need 36 months of history, so the first signal is mid-2008 and the 2003–07 boom is not tested.
  • The RBI stance table is approximate and from memory. The flows pillar has no usable history and is excluded.
Running it each month

Process, data and testing

Monthly routine (about 20 minutes)

  • P/E and P/B for all three indices: NSE index factsheets, or Screener / Trendlyne / indexpe.in.
  • 6-month returns and 200-DMA of the Mid/Nifty and Small/Nifty price ratios: build the ratio in TradingView (NIFTY_MIDCAP_150/NIFTY).
  • Earnings: quarterly result reviews from Motilal Oswal, ICICI Direct or Kotak. Keep to one source.
  • AMFI monthly data for category inflows. RBI policy dates. FII/DII daily data from NSE.
  • Update the calculator, note the scores in a log, and act only when a target moves 5pp or more.

What the backtest changes

  • Use P/B rather than P/E for the valuation pillars on mid and smallcaps.
  • Give momentum and the rate stance little weight; they added nothing over 12 months.
  • Use the score for lump sums and rebalancing decisions, and let SIPs run at the base split.
  • Expect long stretches, like 2021–24, where the model looks wrong while valuations keep rising.

Execution

  • Use low-cost index funds or ETFs (Nifty 50, Midcap 150, Smallcap 250) for the tilts, so the signal is not mixed up with fund-manager skill.
  • Make tilts with new SIP money and STPs first. Sell only when the gap is large.
  • Tax: equity STCG is 20% and LTCG 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year. Prefer rebalancing lots held over 12 months, and check exit loads.

Known weaknesses

  • Index P/E figures differ between providers, and index constituents change over time.
  • Mean reversion can take years. Smallcaps stayed expensive through most of 2021–24.
  • Market cap/GDP has a structural uptrend in India, which is why it carries a small weight.
  • The pillar weights come from reasoning and published studies. The backtest checked them but did not fit them.
This is an educational framework and a worked example, not personalised investment advice. I'm not a SEBI-registered adviser. Several 2026 figures come from aggregators that disagree with each other, so check key inputs against NSE, AMFI and RBI before acting, and consider a registered adviser for decisions about your own money.
